FOUNDATIONS CATALOG — SOURCE AND EDITORIAL NOTES
Prepared 2026-10-30 for the public Builder's Reading Atlas expansion.

Scope and validation
- Exactly 100 nonfiction recommendations: n101–n200.
- 45 Math & learning; 30 Physics & astronomy; 15 Chemistry & materials; 10 Science & discovery.
- JSON schema, sequential unique IDs, difficulty range, HTTPS source URLs, unique normalized titles, and no duplicate titles against the pre-existing catalog were checked with Python.
- Bibliographic titles and author identities were checked against publisher, author/estate, university, OpenStax, or publisher-hosted JSTOR records using web search and targeted page reads. This is a curated recommendation catalog, not a claim that all 100 works were read cover to cover in this session.
- Each why/question/caveat is original editorial writing. Aerospace/mechanical/space-living applications are editorial connections, not statements attributed to the publishers.
- Twenty original publication years are included where the research explicitly supported them. Eighty years remain null rather than mix guessed dates, original dates, revised editions, translations, and paperback publication dates.

Source coverage and limitations
- Sources are primarily individual book pages. A small number use official author bibliographies or book indexes: Strang, Strogatz's Sync, Downey's Think Stats/Think Bayes, Feynman's books, Greene, and Miodownik. These indexes explicitly identify the relevant books.
- How to Study for a Mathematics Degree uses Oxford University Press's author discussion, identifying Lara Alcock and the 2012 UK title. The US title How to Study as a Mathematics Major is the same work and is not counted separately.
- Visual Differential Geometry and Forms uses its complete Princeton University Press record on JSTOR. It was directly opened and title, author, publisher, and 2021 copyright were confirmed.
- OpenStax detail pages returned valid book titles but little machine-readable text. Prefaces were read for deeper confirmation, including corrected contributor lists for Elementary Algebra 2e (three authors) and Intermediate Algebra 2e (Marecek and Mathis).
- Miodownik's author book page was available in indexed primary-source results; a direct read returned a transient 502. Liquid Rules is the US title of Liquid, not a second counted book.
- Catalog/search snippets and accessible primary pages establish bibliographic identity, not exhaustive fact checking of everything within each book. Links can change. No prices, current inventory, or claim of every edition being available were added.
- No summaries are copied from publisher blurbs. Recommended applications, difficulty levels, stages, and caveats are editorial judgments.
- Prealgebra/elementary/intermediate algebra are staged alternatives for closing gaps, not a claim everyone must read all three. Calculus 2/3 and University Physics 2/3 are distinct curricular volumes.
- The Periodic Table is classified as nonfiction memoir with an explicit caveat that it contains fictionalized stories.
- The full Feynman Lectures counts as one work. Six Easy Pieces and Six Not So Easy Pieces were deliberately not added as further entries because they excerpt that work.
- No editions of one title were counted as separate recommendations.
- The existing site already contains core College Algebra, Precalculus, Calculus Volume 1, University Physics Volume 1, Infinite Powers, What Is Mathematics?, How to Solve It, A Mind for Numbers, The Art of Doing Science and Engineering, Strong Materials, and Structures; these are not repeated.

Practical starter suggestions from this addition
- n107 The Joy of x: motivation during foundational mathematics.
- n137 Street-Fighting Mathematics: estimation and dimensional reasoning after some calculus.
- n174 Storm in a Teacup: very accessible physical intuition.
- n180 Stuff Matters: tangible materials thinking.
- n196 The Visual Display of Quantitative Information: communicate project evidence clearly.
- These are choices, not an instruction to buy a large stack of books.
